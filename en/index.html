How it works

When you read the usual way, your eyes make small jumps along the line, slip back and get lost on the page. Lince does that work for you: it delivers each word exactly where you're already looking.

Measure your speed

Fast, but understanding

Reading fast is useless if you don't understand. That's why Lince doesn't push you to go faster: it measures.

  • The speed test combines words per minute with comprehension questions, across 5 different texts.
  • If you're fast but understand little, Lince tells you honestly and suggests slowing down.
  • After each session, "How much did you understand?" adjusts your recommended speed.

What research says (no hype)

This method is called RSVP (rapid serial visual presentation), and it has been studied for decades.

It helps focus

With a single word on screen there's nothing else to look at: no line jumps, no accidental re-reading, and it's harder for your mind to drift.

It has limits

Research shows that at very high speeds comprehension drops. There's no magic shortcut, which is why Lince measures how much you understand and never promises "3× faster reading".

It works best like this

Short sessions, texts you care about, and a comfortable speed you raise little by little.

A thorough review: Rayner, Schotter, Masson, Potter & Treiman (2016), "So Much to Read, So Little Time", Psychological Science in the Public Interest.

Frequently asked questions

Is Lince free?

Yes. You can read without paying and without a time limit. Lince Plus is optional and removes the limits on classics, imports and pasted text.

Will I understand what I read?

That's the point. You start at a comfortable speed and Lince checks your comprehension with short questions. It only suggests going faster when you're understanding well.

Is it tiring to read this way?

It feels different at first. The pauses at punctuation help, and you can make them longer in Settings. We recommend short sessions of 10 to 20 minutes.

Does it help with ADHD or dyslexia?

Many people who get distracted easily find it easier to focus with one word at a time, and Lince includes fonts like OpenDyslexic. But it's not a medical treatment: try it and keep what works for you.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The 50 classics come inside the app and your imported books are stored on your phone. You only need internet to fetch an article, use an online library, sign in or buy Plus.

Which formats can I read?

EPUB, PDF (with real text, not scans) and TXT, plus pasted text, web articles and OPDS catalogs.

Is there an iPhone version?

For now Lince is Android only.

How do I cancel Lince Plus?

In Google Play → your profile → Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions. The one-time purchase doesn't renew.
